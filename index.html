KAREL D. TEMMINK

I study what happens when stars have a companion: how they exchange matter, influence each other’s evolution, and sometimes merge.

The central question is when mass transfer remains stable and when it changes the fate of the binary.

I completed my PhD in computational astrophysics at Radboud University in 2026. I combine detailed stellar models with simulations of whole populations to understand what binary interactions leave behind, including white dwarfs and stellar eruptions.

I like problems where a familiar approximation stops working. I write, run and test numerical models, then try to understand whether a surprising result is telling us something about the physics or the assumptions we made.

I also enjoy teaching, discussing ideas with collaborators, and making difficult results understandable. I've worked with satellite data outside academia, and I'm interested in applying my experience in modelling and analysis to problems beyond astrophysics too.

WHAT I DO

The work behind the science

I move between physical questions, numerical experiments, and the work of making results useful to others.

01

Modelling and code

Developing detailed models with MESA, exploring stellar populations with SeBa and binary_c, and writing analysis and simulation tools in Python and Fortran.

02

Interpreting results

Testing numerical models against one another and against observations, especially when the usual approximations give an unexpected answer.

03

Explaining the work

Teaching, collaborating, documenting research software, and explaining technical work to audiences with different backgrounds.
