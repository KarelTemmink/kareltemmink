Stellar evolution · Numerical modelling

BINARY STARS / NUMERICAL ASTROPHYSICS

Research & publications

When two stars exchange matter, the outcome can reshape both stars and their orbit. I study what decides whether a binary survives, becomes unstable, or merges.

I use detailed stellar-evolution models with MESA to test the simplified rules in faster population-synthesis codes such as SeBa and binary_c. My work involves large simulation grids, Python and Fortran tools, and comparisons with observed white dwarfs, post-interaction binaries and stellar transients.

A recurring theme is finding out where a convenient approximation stops being reliable. The projects below approach that problem from three directions.

RESEARCH THEMES

Questions I work on

01↗

BINARY EVOLUTION

When does mass transfer become unstable?

Mass transfer does not always end in a common envelope or merger. I use detailed models to find when a donor star can keep losing matter steadily, and how the answer changes when matter also escapes from the binary.

Stability study, 2023 ↗Mass loss study, 2026 ↗
02✶

STELLAR TRANSIENTS

Can we recognise a merger before it happens?

Some binary stars approach instability over years or decades. I model changes in their brightness and colour that might reveal the approach of a merger, and work with observers to interpret stellar eruptions and common-envelope interactions.

Research paper, 2025 ↗
03◎

STELLAR POPULATIONS

What histories are hidden in white dwarfs?

A white dwarf that looks like a single star may be the remnant of a merger. Population models can estimate how often this happens and how it affects the ages we infer for these stars.

Research paper, 2020 ↗
